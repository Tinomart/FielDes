# Known issues

What you should know before you spend time on something. A problem that is not here: **Help ▸ Report a bug** opens a new issue with the version and your system filled in.
What a tool does and does not do (its scope, its accuracy, its cost) is in the guide, next to the tool: [docs/manufacturing.md](docs/manufacturing.md), [docs/analysis.md](docs/analysis.md),
[docs/fields.md](docs/fields.md), [docs/interface.md](docs/interface.md); the README has the design decisions (*Known limitations*).

## Platforms

- **Windows** is the main platform. The setup program and the zip are not signed: Windows SmartScreen may say *unknown publisher* the first time; *More info ▸ Run anyway*
  opens it (the source is public, and the setup is built from it by the script in `scripts/`).
- **Linux** packages (a folder, a `.run` installer, a `.deb`) are built on Ubuntu 22.04 (glibc 2.35), so they open on Ubuntu 22.04, Debian 12 and everything newer. They were run under WSL2
  (Ubuntu 22.04, with WSLg), not on every distribution.
- There is **no macOS** build.

## Meshing and analysis

Found by running the validation again on the new mesher (the numbers are on [docs/validation.md](docs/validation.md)); none of them is hidden by a tolerance, and each is an open issue of the mesher, not a limit of the method:

- **A part that is only two elements thin loses section.** A round wire 1.95 mm across meshed with 1 mm elements holds 6.9 % too little of its volume and its natural frequencies read 3.3 to 4.3 % low; with 0.8 mm elements they are exact within 0.4 %. The passes that put nodes on edges take a curve this coarse for a crease. Halve the element size and see whether the answer moves.
- **A symmetric part is meshed on one side and mirrored, and the symmetry is decided on the field before anything is meshed** (the part and its mirror image overlapped, the difference searched with interval arithmetic, once; a plane where the surface meets it at a shallow angle is not used): the real bracket of example 05 is mirrored at 2, 3.75 and 6.8 mm and at ten times and 0.01 times its size, the frying pan about two planes, the mobile stand about none. **One fault is known:** where a wall of the part runs PARALLEL to the plane within an element or so (the shaft stand at 6.3 mm: 54 plane vertices 0.8 of an element off the wall) the mirrored mesh is accepted with those vertices off the surface and its largest stress is 2.1 where the whole part's is 0.96. If a symmetric part with a wall close to its middle plane gives a stress that changes when the element size changes by a tenth, mesh it at another size and compare.
- **A few rows of faces on the real bracket still lean off the surface** (0.3 % of the skin at 2 mm): four faces where the rim of the large circle crosses the mirror plane, and rows of five to eight faces where three surfaces meet within a millimetre or two (the inside corner of the foot, the junction of the ring and the neck). They are visible only at grazing angles along the edge. A straight edge that crosses the mirror plane has its node at the crossing (the step that a plain box had in each long edge is gone); a curved rim that crosses it does not get that.
- **The skin of `mesh_cartesian` still follows the surface less closely than the tetrahedra's** (found 2026-11-15, improved the same day: small cut cells are joined instead of left out, every node of the skin is put on the surface, the tetrahedra's edge pass and collapse are done for the cut cells): on the real bracket 6.6 % of its skin faces lean more than 12 degrees from the surface at 4 mm and 2.5 % at 2 mm (the tetrahedra: 3.7 % and 0.3 %; before: 22.5 % and 14.1 %). The ones that are left lie where three surfaces meet within a cube or two (the ring-neck junction, the inside corner of the foot) and on fillets smaller than the element, where there is no crease to put a node on: of the nodes of the leaning faces about half have no crease within 0.6 of a cube. In the window the creases of the bracket's neck are straight to 0.001 mm on both sides of its top face (every node on a crease lies on the exact line); what still shows under them, now that the join threshold is 4 (Salome's quarter) and not 10, is much less: the thin dark rows of cells along the neck edge, 0.1 to 0.3 of a cube thick, are mostly gone in the window at 2 mm (the faces that lean are 2.2 %, the largest cell over the smallest 6.8 where it was 15); the outer ring of an arm end is toothed on one side, where it runs into the neck: a fan of pointed triangles round one node, in a blend that is smaller than the cell (a step of 1.4 mm at 2 mm elements), so it is a limit of the resolution and not of a pass; and hexahedral cells refuse to move their nodes onto a crease the way tetrahedra can (at 2 mm a neighbouring cell would fall under 0.7 of its volume 398 times, a scaled Jacobian under 0.3 316 times). The cells are less regular than a mesh that leaves the skin where the grid put it: at 2 mm 0.66 % of them have a skewness by face angles over 0.85 (0.13 % before), 38 of 38,984 over 0.95 (polygons with a node at 177 to 180 degrees that is not quite on the line of its neighbours), the mean orthogonal quality 0.96 (0.98), the longest edge over the shortest of a cell up to 13 (13). Two more passes of the tetrahedra were tried for the cut cells and made the faces lean more, so they are not in: the rim of a flat face (`snapRims`) and the even spacing of the nodes of a straight crease (`smoothChains`).
