First written on the night of 2026-10-16, rewritten on 2026-10-19 by Claude, for the maintainer. Nothing here has been pushed, tagged or published.
The release candidate is labelled **0.10.0** in the file names only: the version in `CMakeLists.txt` is still 0.6.1 (you bump it), and the packages were built with a temporary 0.10.0 that was put back afterwards.

The last pushed state is `origin/main` d59e380 (the 0.9.0 beta, 2026-10-14). ## 2. The features of the release

1. **A call: a field asked at a computed point, without a copy of it** (kernel, Python `s(x', y', z')`). Values, derivatives (so dragging a handle), sharp-edge features, interval pruning and saving agree with the substitution it replaces to 2.4e-7.
   The copies of `smooth`, the images of a symmetry, the placement of STEP and mesh imports and the lookups of manufacturing are calls now (the bracket's optimization: 29 s and 307 MB where it was 97 s and 3.9 GB). A crash of the mesher on eight threads (a neighbour with no leaf) is fixed.
   *Evidence:* the equality tests and example 29's three legs (earlier); all examples that place copies drew on 10-16 (example 27 and 29 included).
2. **Imported meshes are drawn in seconds, not a minute.** Example 12 (232,764 triangles): 48.8 s to about 1.7 s in the window. *Evidence:* measured earlier; example 12 drew with the rest on 10-16.
3. **The evaluator.** Passes of 8 to 192 points run with block sizes the compiler knows (both interpreters), the row of the value array is 17 cache lines, and the "is this corner ambiguous" check runs only for a corner whose value is exactly 0. Same mesh, vertex for vertex. Tried and not kept: a 4-point edge search for every field that calls others (23 % more vertices, vertices up to 0.25 mm off the surface).
4. **Manufacturing:** constraints (`manufacturing_constraint`, `fff_constraint`, `cnc_constraint`, `molding_constraint`), the check (`manufacturing_analysis`: every rule is a field drawn on the part, and a report), and the shell (`manufacturing_optimization`: thickens thin walls, closes or widens gaps, rounds inside corners, puts a draft on, fills undercuts, makes overhangs stand with a gusset or a teardrop), supports (`support='block'`), `export_print` (3MF), `export_layers` (DXF), `export_slice` (SVG), free build direction, `.oriented()`, `max_change`, `keep=`, a second pass.
   **The check is independent of the shell** (four files; the check imports nothing of the optimization, and measures a wall or a gap with its own method) and `mfg_11` shows it catching a fix that is wrong.
   *Evidence:* section 1 (the cases, the proofs), `tests/manufacturing/00_START_HERE.py` (one line per case with the result of its last run), `tests/manufacturing/JOB_TESTS.md` (a real part made ready for a printer, a mill and a mould, judged against published rules).
5. **The result card of a manufacturing result** (new on 10-19). The blocks of support are the fourth entry of the legend ("print supports", amber) and no longer a second card of their own; the card counts **every** place the re-check found (it counted only the five biggest of each rule: 1.9 % of the surface where 6.9 % were found on the moulding sampler; `.fail_totals` has them all); and a result whose way up was left free says it: the row "Way up of the print" with the direction the tool found, and `.oriented()` is the part turned to stand that way (`mfg_b1` draws it beside the original). In the six languages.
6. **Parametric optimization** (`parametric_optimization`): the best numbers for the `var()`s of a model over any analysis; `apply=True` writes them into the text. `Result.mass` (kg) and `ModalResult.volume` / `.mass` are new for it. *10-16:* six proofs against known answers.
7. **Engineering:** a right-click menu of its own with a category and colour for each domain (structural, thermal, fluid). Earlier; the menu was dumped and pictured in the UI smoke of 10-16.
8. **10-node tetrahedra** (`element='tet10'`) for static and modal analysis: the cantilever -0.8 % with 1,296 elements where 66,816 linear ones read -2.0 %; the plate with a hole +0.4 % with 45,000 elements (one eighth of the plate, by symmetry). *10-16* (verification v1 and v2).
9. **Four known answers and a notice:** `examples/verification/` stops with an error when FielDes is farther from the answer than a tolerance; the result card says *Not validated for safety-critical use*. *10-16.*
10. **Settings > Navigation:** Blender style and Touchpad as options beside the default (Shift + left drag stays). Earlier; the presets were switched in the UI smoke of 10-16.
11. **Help > Report a bug** opens a prefilled GitHub issue (nothing is sent from the program); `KNOWN_ISSUES.md`. Earlier; the dialog was opened in the UI smoke of 10-16.
12. **The pane that has the keyboard has a frame, and the status bar says *Keys: viewport* or *Keys: editor*.** Earlier. Turning the view says that it closed the work plane view.
13. **The guided tour is one walk of 32 steps** after the welcome card, with a lattice step; a skipped tour says where it is found. Earlier; walked step by step in the UI smoke of 10-16.
14. **`sketch` is now `profile`** (no alias; a beta change), and **round corners** (`point(x, y, z, radius=4)`: an exact arc, the arc is the handle of the radius). Earlier; examples 26, 28 and 29 drew on 10-16.
15. **Cold against warm:** all 31 examples agreed when run with empty caches and again with warm ones (2026-10-14); the examples README says how long each takes.
16. **Packaging and documents:** packaging from the one build tree (`scripts/build-windows.ps1 -Package`), the rules of symmetry written down (`docs/interface.md`), a render strip in the README, the known-issues list, 50 stale translations removed on 10-14.
17. **A fixed crash at exit:** closing the program after a script error (a syntax error is enough) ended in a corrupted heap (Windows exit code 0xC0000374); the window's error handlers wrote into a viewport and a tree that were already destroyed. They hold them by guards now.
18. **A fixed primitive:** `cone(radius, height, base)` drew a cone 2.3 times too big (its base radius came out sqrt(R^2 + H^2)). No example uses it; found by a test that counts each primitive's volume on points against its formula.
19. **The derivative of a field, as a field** (`Shape.partial(axis)`, kernel `libfive_tree_partial`, commit fb446c3; 10-19). The symbolic derivative of the expression by the rules of calculus: the gradient the manufacturing tool reads its normals from used to be a difference of two calls, whose interval bounds in the mesher are as many times too wide as a cell is wider than the step (12 times at 3 cells/mm). *Evidence (10-19, real windows):* against central differences of the field on 12 fields (a box, a union of boxes, a sphere, calls of calls with a `sin` in the arguments, abs / square / division, min / max mixes, powers and roots, tan / asin / acos / atan, atan2 / exp / log / sin / cos): at most 0.43 % of 3,000 points differ, those within a step of a kink, no NaN (a first version gave NaN inside every box: the square root of the exact box distance is a root of exactly 0 there, and 0/0 read as 0 now); the normals, the chord and the face test of the wall remedy at eight points of a fin are identical to those by differences; example 33's part: 3244.9 mm^3 added where the reference is 3245. *What it did NOT do:* it did not make example 33 draw faster (the cells of the mesher are the same, 61,842 interval cells and 210,416 leaves, with it and without), and the first timing that said so (11 s against 47 s) was the NaN above switching the wall remedy off: written down as a lesson, a speed-up with a changed volume is a switched-off feature.
20. **The mesher skips ties of equal derivatives** (10-19). Where a vertex is put on an edge the mesher asks whether the point is ambiguous (a min or a max with two sides of exactly the same value) and searches the features there; on example 33's part 165,069 of 178,426 intersection points (92.5 %) were such ties, nearly all of them `max(0, 0)` (a clamp of a value that is exactly its bound: the same derivative on both sides, nothing to choose), and the check of the values ran every called field again at each. A tie now counts where the derivatives of the two sides differ, noted by the derivative pass itself. *Evidence:* ambiguous points 165,069 to 40,098, the ambiguity check 26.5 s to 0, the search of features 65.7 to 17.4 thread-seconds, the work of the leaves 415 to 331, the draw 65.6 to 54.0 s; the same mesh (34,032 collected cells) and the same volume.
21. **`exact_distance`, `offset_exact`, `shell_exact`, `round_edges` and `fillet` read the shape's own field** (nothing is meshed; 10-19, by your order that a field-driven tool does not work on a mesh made from the field). Newton steps along the derivative: exact for a field that is a distance, at an edge or corner of planes at any angle the distance to the edge line or the corner point, a step believed only as far as the face it leads to is there, a slot or wall narrower than the ball found by a walk that turns back on itself. *Evidence (10-19, real windows, hand answers):* distance to a face / edge / corner / inside of a box 5.0000 / 5.0012 / 7.0720 / -2.0000 (5, 5, 7.0711, -2); a box offset by 2: 2609.3 mm^3 (2610.5); a box with its edges rounded by 2: 909.8 (907.7); the fillet of an L (radius 3): 15.51 mm^3 added in 8 mm of corner (15.45), drawn in 1.9 s (the version that read the gradient by differences ran out of 8 GB and did not draw it in 8 minutes); a slot 2 wide under a ball of radius 2 filled to 285.00 mm^3 (285.00); a plate thinner than the ball removed whole (0.0000 left); about 1.3 microseconds a point. **Where it is not as exact as the old mesh version, which passed the corner cases of `tests/manufacturing`:** see section 6 (corner cases c1 to c5 and a3).
22. **A distance field is drawn from the field it was made from** (commit d76eb70; 10-19). The walk of `exact_distance` has the sign of the field everywhere (its value is the field plus a correction held on the field's side of zero), so its surface is the field's, but meshing it costs about 32 calls of the field at every point of the drawing: `exact_distance(block)` of six pockets (`mfg_a3_pocket_corners`) took the mesher 309 s at its second finest level and the check's display of it 334 s, and the case hit its 530 s cap. A shape made by `exact_distance` now says which field it is drawn from (`_display_field`, carried by `move`, `rotate`, `scale`, `reflect` and `colored`; read in `interpreter.cpp`); what a tool, a field viewer or an offset reads is still the walk. *Evidence (10-19, real window, pictures looked at):* the same `exact_distance(block)` meshes in 0.4 s at the finest level, the check's display of it in 10.6 s, the case runs in 145 s with 818 MB (the mesh version: 110 to 147 s).
23. **The model tree no longer expands what it cannot count** (commit d76eb70; 10-19). After every run the tree and the handles counted the draggable numbers of each row with the unshared `Tree::flatten`, exponential for a field made of calls that take calls: a part held as `exact_distance` took **8.2 GB within 19 s of the end of the script**. `Tree::flatten(share, calls, budget)` takes a budget of two million steps for the tree. *Evidence:* the same script stays at about 300 MB (memory probe, 10-19).
24. **The overhang remedy is a field of the kernel, made on a lattice** (`kernel/src/fields/lattice_field.cpp`, `libfive_field_overhang_lattice`; 10-19, by your word "if you think it is the best option go with it"). The formula that read the part ~100 times a point is gone: the part is sampled once on a lattice (pitch = the larger of a 250th of the part and the cube root of its volume over 1.2e7), the overhang is made on the lattice, and the result is a field of one lookup. Topology-optimized bracket, whole flow at element 5: the tool 200 s to 16-19 s, the first complete drawing 46 s, the window exits 0 at 1.2 GB (it was ~6 min and a crash at 8 GB). *Evidence:* the overhang proofs in real windows (o1 9/9, o2 10/10, o3 8/8, o4 14/14, o6 8/8, o7 3/3, o8 3/3, s1 9/9, s2 9/9) and pictures of the bracket from above and below. *Limit, said on the card and in KNOWN_ISSUES:* a gusset thinner than the lattice pitch is made too small (a 47-degree sliver: 2.35 mm^3 against 6.6 at a fine pitch).
25. **Cut-plane colours** (`slice.frag`, `view.cpp`; 10-19; you asked "what is the green and black"). The section's colours were a shader colour table that read NaN in the shader, which is not reliable; the colours are a table made from the colour map and a sentinel of 1e30. *Evidence:* the section pictures of the overhang cases after the change.
26. **The walls and gaps remedies draw in seconds** (`manufacturing.py` `_clip`, `manufacturing_field.py` `_expensive_part`; 10-19). The remedies call the part with offsets built from its own value and normal; in a cell where its interval is loose the callee tape cannot be shortened. Every such offset is clamped to the longest one that is ever read, only where the part is expensive to read (more than 15 microseconds a point near its surface: the bracket 27-30, a plate 1). A 30 mm window onto the bracket: 190 to 17 thread-seconds, 8-19 s to 1.8 s; the CNC sampler no longer takes 6.5 GB (it took 991 MB without the clamp, 994 MB with the policy). The exact 8-probe chord stays (a one-lookup ball formula that I tried failed 5 of the 16 walls and gaps proofs and was removed; all 16 pass).
27. **The card's colours say what the tool did** (`ManufacturingResult._display`; 10-19). A result that only added material shows no blue, one that only cut shows no red, whatever a vertex a fraction of a cell off the surface reads; a result is drawn as one body, coloured by the change at its surface, when at least half of what changed is thin layers.
28. **The ball of an inside corner reads the walls it meets** (`distance_field._ball_walk`, `_gate`; 10-19; you showed the CNC sampler and said it looked completely wrong, and it was). `closing` and `opening` (the library's `fillet` and `round_edges`, the corners remedy) walked three Newton steps to the centres of the ball and called a point "not reached by a ball" when two steps ran against each other: over a block lower than the wall beside it the steps bounce between the wall and the block's corner and the gap was filled up to 14.5 mm where the ball leaves everything above 13; and the gate that shuts the construction on flat surfaces was fooled where two kinks cancel exactly (a sheet of air 1 mm wide next to the fin). The walk now ends at the point on the planes of the walls it met (up to three, any angle), leaves out a step to a wall that is not there (the cutter overshoots the part), follows the path of the steps only where the planes do not verify themselves and the path arrived at the centres (a curve tighter than the ball), and is left out where it does not verify itself; the gate reads unequal steps. Measured against the exact closing in labs (3D, difference-made parts with overshooting cutters, rounded pocket corners, slots, V-grooves) and in real windows: see the table in section 3. *Limits, said in KNOWN_ISSUES:* the cap at the mouth of a slot narrower than the ball is filled flush with its rim; a curve tighter than the ball is filled to the end faces only where the field is a distance (1-2 mm short of the mouth of a pocket cut by an overshooting cutter); a slot 2 % narrower than the ball counts as fitting.
29. **The three sampler cases now carry hand answers** (`mfg_i1`, `mfg_i2`, `mfg_i3`). They asked for "any" change, which is why a wrong picture passed. i2: the two gaps solid up to 12 (the second at 1 mm from the L up to 10.5: the ball's arc is at 12.07 there and a fill over a shoulder is exact to about 1 mm) and nothing above 14.8, the hole of 5 mm filled in the middle and open at both ends by the sag of the ball on its rims (1.34 mm; my first hand answer said "filled all through" and was wrong, the window showed the exact closing), the foot of a wall 0.76 mm high at 1 mm from it, i3: the boss (a cylinder) a cone, 0.349 mm out at z = 7 and 0.210 at z = 11 at eight angles; i3: the draft stands 0.349 mm out of a wall at z = 7; i1: the fin 0.15 mm thicker each side, the gap of 0.3 filled, the hole open.
30. **A moulding result is drawn as one coloured body, and the card says what each remedy added** (`manufacturing.py`; 10-19; you showed the moulding sampler: "random spikes on the cylinder, not an actual nice raft", "the thickening of the lower plate ... extends across the edges like playdough, looks really weird and poor, I dont know the purpose"). Measured first: the field was right (the boss, radius 5, stands out of its wall by 0.402 mm at z = 5.5, 0.349 at 7, 0.210 at 11, 0.087 at 14.5, the same at all 24 angles round it), the picture was not: the draft is a skin that tapers to nothing at the top of its wall and was not marked thin, so the result was drawn as three bodies and the red skin, thinner than a cell of 0.5 mm, as spikes. The draft is a thin skin now (one body, coloured by the change at each point of the surface) and the card has one row for each remedy that was kept ("Draft applied to walls, 1035 mm3") so that it says what the added material is for. *Open, asked to you:* the draft is measured from the top of the whole part, so a low wall (the 5 mm plate) is thickened by 0.4 to 0.6 mm all the way up and steps out past its own top edge; measured from each wall's own top edge it would be 0.17 mm at the foot and nothing at the top.
31. **A fixed crash: the window ran to 8 GB when the mouse first rested on the thin layer of a three-body manufacturing result** (`shape.cpp` `Shape::buildDeps`; 10-19). Found by replaying the sampler's picture sequence in six windows (not the zoom, not the colour probe under the cursor, which reads at once): every hover asks `nativeDragOk`, which listed the variables of the shape under the mouse with the unshared inline `flatten()` -- for the red layer the whole remedy chain, the part called dozens of times -- on the GUI thread. It reads the shared flatten with the calls kept (`walk_deep`): the same variables, in a time that does not grow with every call.

## 6. Honest notes

- **Speed: the total time of a result is the tool's seconds plus the seconds to its first complete drawing, added.** Measured on this machine (a 1.3 GHz laptop processor, no spare core; run-to-run differences of up to 15 %), at 3 cells to the millimetre:

  | | before | now |
  |---|---|---|
  | the ledge with its gusset (`mfg_03`, left half) | 5.9 + 72.3 s = 78 s | 1.3 to 1.6 + 36 to 44 s = about 40 s |
  | the block with a round hole and its teardrop (`mfg_03`, right half) | 16.2 + 49.7 s = 66 s | 1.2 + 29 to 36 s = about 33 s |
  | the whole proof `mfg_03` | draw 227 to 280 s | draw 180 s |
  | the FFF sampler, the CNC sampler, the moulding sampler (2 cells/mm) | | 6.1 + 29 s, 5.8 + 35 s, 5.0 + 12 s |

  **This is faster, not fast.** What is left is the work itself: a point of the field of a result is tens of lookups of the part, and the wall remedy of this version draws about three times slower than that of the first release candidate (a fin of 0.6 mm: 16 s where it was 5 s) because it carries the later correctness fixes (about 20 lookups of the part for every cell near the fin). Example 33 (a fin, a post and a ledge) draws in 54.0 s after a tool of 8 s (10-19, after item 20; 65.6 s before it): the cells are the same with the derivative and without (61,842 in the interval pass, 210,416 leaves, 77,092 of them on the surface), the cost is per cell: of the 331 thread-seconds of leaf work 153 are derivatives, 107 the search along edges, 52 the corners and 17 the features, and 32 called fields stand on the tape of an average leaf. Drawing such a part at 2 cells to the millimetre is about four times faster. This is the next thing to work on: fewer lookups in the wall remedy, or fewer points where a vertex is searched.
- **A part held as `exact_distance` is slow through the tool and in the drawing of its result, and the bracket jobs (`mfg_x1_topology_bracket`, `mfg_j1`, `mfg_j2`, `mfg_j7`) were therefore not run.** Measured on 10-19 (real windows, same machine). A small part (plate, fin, post, wall, ledge; printer rules; 3 cells/mm) gives the same result every way (483.3 mm^3 added): the plain part takes **6.7 s in the tool and 8.5 s to draw**; the part held as a one-step light distance (a prototype, not in the program) 36 s and 248 s; held as `exact_distance` **295 s and not finished after 20 minutes** (a smaller `reach` changes nothing). Example 33's part held as `exact_distance`: tool 282 s (plain 17 s, the old mesh version 195 s), the drawing of the result at 4 % after 35 minutes (plain 54 s, mesh version 312 s). Why: one lookup of the distance is about 32 calls of the part's field (three Newton steps of a value and three partial derivatives each, the foot tests at both ends of each step, the gate), the tool asks the part 20 to 120 times at every point of its result, and the tool's own re-check of the result asks the result about 50 times at each of its samples (264 of the 295 s). **The real bracket:** one point of the topology-optimized shape costs 19.7 microseconds (example 33's boxes: 1.0; the density is a native FEA field with no symbolic derivative, `.max(domain)` is the STEP part's heavy field), the walk 388, a one-step distance 141; handed to the tool as it is (no `exact_distance`) the tool's remedies took 160 s and the re-check of its result 640 s. The old flow was fast only because `exact_distance` meshed the field, which turned the 20-microsecond field into a distance object of a few microseconds; that is the thing your order of 10-19 forbids, and there is no field-only equivalent. Ideas not done: the partial derivative with a step for the native field, a re-check limited to the neighbourhood of what the remedies changed, a kernel call kind whose derivative is not taken (the mesher's derivative through the walk is a second derivative), fewer lookups in the remedies. Showing `exact_distance(part)` itself is not slow (item 22).
- **The corner cases of `tests/manufacturing` do not all pass with the field-only fillet (10-19, commit fb446c3, real windows, pictures looked at).** The old fillet meshed the part and passed all six; the new one rolls its ball in the field and is as exact as the field is a distance. Clean geometry is exact (item 21); the cases are built with cutters that overshoot the block and with unions of overlapping pieces, whose fields are only a lower bound of the distance, and there it is less exact. Last run of each (the numbers are the case's own): **c2** (corners of 60, 90 and 120 degrees): the three arcs are right to 0.15 mm (the check that the filled corner is the arc of the ball); **c1** (an L, radius 6): 2 of 8 fail, the material of the corner reaches 0.4 mm past the end faces of the part, and the independent check marks 1.5 % of the surface; **c5** (a boss on a plate): 1 of 7, the independent check marks 0.7 %; **c3** (three faces, rounded pockets): 3 of 15, the blind pocket's spheres and the pocket whose corners are already 5 mm pass, the pocket of 1.5 mm corners made 3 mm gets 38 mm^3 where the arcs say 69; **c4** (a slot narrower than the ball): 4 of 10, the slot is filled whole and flush with its mouth where the ball resting on its two edges would leave a sag of 0.76 mm, and the wide slot gets 66 mm^3 where 77 are right; **a3** (the check of `exact_distance(block)` of a part with six pockets): it ran out of 8 GB and its time cap on the first run of the night, and runs in 145 s with 818 MB since the two fixes of items 22 and 23; 6 of its 7 checks pass (the corners of 0, 1 and 1.8 mm are marked, those of 3 and 5 mm are not, the outside corners are never marked) and the seventh, that the report lists regions in the first four pockets and in no other, fails because of 0.4 % of the surface along the top and bottom rim of the pockets: within about 1.3 mm of the open end of a through pocket the cutter that overshoots the block leaves the field as the distance to the cutter's end cap, so the distance at the centre of the ball reads 1.9 mm at 0.2 mm from the rim and 3.0 from 2 mm on (the mesh version marked none). **c2** also has one floating point of added material (at z 10.1 on a rim, 1 point) and 19.8 % of its surface marked by the independent check after the fix (arcs meeting faces).
